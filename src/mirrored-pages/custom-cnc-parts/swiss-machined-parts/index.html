What Are Swiss
Machined Parts?

Swiss machined parts are precision turned components produced on Swiss-type CNC machines. Unlike conventional turning, Swiss machining supports the material close to the cutting point, reducing deflection and improving stability for small, slender and complex Swiss turned parts.

This process is especially suitable for precision Swiss CNC components with tight tolerances, long length-to-diameter ratios and multiple features such as threads, grooves, cross-holes, flats and milled details.

  • Guide-bushing support for Swiss turning Material held close to the cut, minimising deflection on small-diameter Swiss turned parts.
  • Precision Swiss CNC components Tolerances down to ±0.005 mm across repeat production, depending on geometry and material.
  • Multi-feature Swiss machined parts Threads, grooves, cross-holes and flats can be completed in one cycle without re-fixturing.
  • Long L/D ratio components Ideal for slender shafts, pins and other turned components that challenge conventional lathes.

Is Swiss Machining Right
for Your Part?

Swiss machining solves specific problems that conventional CNC turning struggles with. If your part needs small-diameter turning, stable geometry or several precision features, it is a strong candidate for Swiss machining.

01

Small diameter

Part diameter under Ø25 mm — especially under Ø6 mm where conventional lathes lose rigidity and dimensional control.

02

High length-to-diameter ratio

Slender shafts or pins where L/D > 3:1 — guide-bushing support keeps the part stable at the cutting point, preventing deflection.

03

Tight tolerances required

Applications demanding ±0.005 mm or tighter on diameter, runout or position, subject to engineering review and feature geometry.

04

Multiple features on one part

Parts with threads, grooves, cross-holes, or flats that would need re-fixturing on a conventional lathe — completed in a single cycle here.

05

High repeatability across batches

OEM components where dimensional consistency from piece to piece and batch to batch is critical to downstream assembly.

06

Difficult-to-hold materials

Brass, stainless, titanium, or free-machining alloys where chip control and surface finish are harder to manage at small diameters.

Finishing Options for
Swiss Machined Parts

In addition to machining, we support finishing and secondary processes that improve corrosion resistance, conductivity, cleanliness, wear performance, and appearance.

Deburring & Polishing

Removes sharp edges and surface burrs from machined parts. Improves handling safety and prepares surfaces for downstream finishing or assembly.

Passivation

Electrochemical treatment for stainless steel parts. Removes free iron from the surface and enhances the natural oxide layer for improved corrosion resistance.

Electropolishing

Electrochemical process that removes a thin surface layer, resulting in ultra-smooth, bright finishes. Commonly specified for medical and semiconductor components.

Plating

Electroplated coatings including nickel, tin, and gold. Improves conductivity, corrosion protection, solderability, and contact reliability for connector and electronic parts.

Anodizing

Electrochemical surface treatment for aluminum parts. Builds a hard, corrosion-resistant oxide layer. Available in natural and colored finishes depending on project requirements.

Black Oxide & Other Finishes

Black oxide for mild corrosion protection and appearance on steel parts. Additional project-specific finishes available — confirm requirements at the quoting stage.

Surface finish requirements often affect part performance as much as machining quality. We review finishing specifications together with geometry, tolerance, and material during the quote stage.
