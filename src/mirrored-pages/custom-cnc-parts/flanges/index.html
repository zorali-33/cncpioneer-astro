What Is
Flange Machining?

Flange machining produces pipe connections, equipment mounts, structural interfaces, and specialty fittings through turning, face milling, boring, drilling, and threading. The process controls sealing-face finish, bolt pattern, bore alignment, and overall dimensions for ASME and custom applications.

Three features govern performance: a flat, correctly finished sealing face for gasket compression; an accurate bolt circle for even clamping; and controlled bore-to-face alignment for clean installation. CNCPioneer plans these dimensions together, not as separate operations.

  • Single-setup MAZAK mill-turn machining Face, bore, bolt circle, hub, and thread features can be machined in one setup to protect bore-to-face and bolt-circle alignment. MAZAK covers bores from 10mm to 500mm; Swiss CNC supports miniature flanges from 0.5mm to 32mm.
  • Multi-material flange capability Machine 6061-T6 and 7075-T6 aluminium, 316L, 304, 17-4PH, C3604 brass, titanium, Inconel, carbon steel, and selected specialty materials.
  • High-precision face and bore control Typical capability includes 0.005mm face flatness, 0.01mm/100mm bore-to-face perpendicularity, ±0.02mm bolt-circle position, and Ra 0.8µm or Ra 0.4µm sealing finishes. Serrated faces can meet ASME B16.5 125–250 AARH.
  • Competitive China production cost Qualified Asian material sourcing and efficient production can reduce cost by 40–60% versus comparable Western suppliers, without changing the planned tolerance, PMI, or documentation scope.

Flange Machining Process
& Capabilities

A controlled production sequence from incoming material verification through machining, finishing, and final inspection, with PPAP or FAIR records when required.

01 · PHASE 1

Material Incoming PMI Inspection

XRF PMI and certificate review confirm alloy identity before production, with hardness, dual certification, and aerospace heat-treatment documentation checked where the programme requires them.

02 · PHASE 2

Rough & Finish Machining

Rough machining establishes the bore or OD datum before finish boring, face turning, and hub profiling. Typical targets include ±0.005mm bores, 0.005mm face flatness, Ra 0.8µm or Ra 0.4µm, ASME B16.5 serrations, and ASME B16.20 RTJ grooves where specified.

03 · PHASE 3

Bolt Circle Drilling & Thread Machining

Bolt circles are drilled with face and bore datums in the same setup when possible. Standard or high-precision BCD targets can be ±0.05mm or ±0.02mm; NPT, BSPT, BSPF, metric, UNC, and UNF threads are verified with calibrated gauges.

04 · PHASE 4

Surface Treatment

Stainless flanges can receive ASTM A967 passivation or electropolishing; aluminium can receive MIL-A-8625 Type III hard anodise or MIL-DTL-5541 chemical film; brass can receive electroless nickel or tin. Finish choice follows corrosion, wear, conductivity, sealing, and dimensional-fit needs.

05 · MATERIALS

Flange Machining Materials

Selection follows the service environment, pressure, strength, temperature, and cost.

06 · DOCUMENTATION

IATF 16949 / AS9100D Documentation

Automotive programmes can include PPAP Level 3 with APQP, FMEA, MSA, Cpk targets, and control plans; aerospace programmes can include AS9102 FAIR. PMI, passivation, electropolishing, C of C, and traceability records are available and retained for at least 15 years.

Materials for Flange
Machining

Choose material by corrosion resistance, pressure strength, temperature, machining response, weldability, sealing-face requirements, and ASME, ASTM, or API compliance. 316L is widely used for process service, ASTM A105 for standard pipe flanges, and 6061-T6 for lightweight applications.

Stainless Steel

316L / ASTM A182 F316L

Process-industry stainless with 2–3% molybdenum for better pitting and crevice corrosion resistance than 304, plus low carbon for welded assemblies. Dedicated machining controls and PMI can prevent 304/316L mix-ups.

Stainless Steel

304 / 304L

General corrosion-resistant choice for atmospheric, water, and mild chemical service when 316L is unnecessary. It typically lowers material cost versus 316L; 304L supports welded assemblies with lower sensitisation risk.

Stainless Steel

17-4PH H900

Precipitation-hardened stainless for high-strength flanges that also need corrosion resistance. Suitable for aerospace hydraulic systems, marine propulsion shafts, and precision mounts subject to combined pressure and structural load.

Aluminum

6061-T6

General lightweight flange alloy with strong machining performance and good corrosion resistance when anodised. Its low density suits mass-sensitive aerospace and automotive hydraulic, fuel, and pneumatic systems.

Aluminum

7075-T6

High-strength aluminium for weight-critical aerospace structures and high-load robot mounts. It can reduce required section size where 6061-T6 cannot provide enough yield strength at the mass target.

Carbon Steel

ASTM A105 Carbon Steel

Cost-effective structural material for high-volume ASME B16.5 pipe flanges in non-corrosive service, including Class 150 through 2500. Blasting, zinc phosphate, and epoxy can support outdoor or buried installations.

Brass

C3604 Free-Cutting Brass

Highly machinable brass for instrumentation ports, pressure gauges, pneumatic manifolds, and plumbing valves. It takes electroless nickel and tin well, with good corrosion resistance for indoor industrial and plumbing service.

Brass

DZR CuZn36Pb2As Brass

Dezincification-resistant brass for warm seawater environments where ordinary brass can suffer selective zinc leaching. Well suited to marine cooling-water and engine-room plumbing flanges requiring long service life.

Copper Alloy

C17200 Beryllium Copper

High-strength, conductive, non-sparking copper alloy for precision instruments and pressure fittings. Suitable for explosive-atmosphere instrument flanges, electronic contact carriers, and high-cycle pneumatic valve bodies.

Titanium

Grade 2 / Ti-6Al-4V

Exceptional corrosion resistance in nitric acid, wet chlorine, and seawater. Grade 2 suits chemical and marine service; Ti-6Al-4V suits high-strength aerospace fuel systems where its performance justifies higher machining cost.

High-Temperature

Inconel 625 / 718

High-temperature nickel alloys that need dedicated tooling and conservative cutting. Inconel 625 suits sour gas, subsea, and concentrated chemical service; Inconel 718 suits aerospace engine and high-temperature reactor flanges.

Engineering Polymer

PEEK

Chemical-resistant engineering polymer for HF and strong-acid service, electrical isolation between dissimilar metals, food-grade non-metallic equipment, and halogen-resistant semiconductor gas systems. Capability can include ±0.005mm bores and 0.008mm face flatness.

316L is a common process-industry choice; ASTM A105 suits high-volume non-corrosive pipe service; 6061-T6 supports lightweight aerospace, automotive, and automation flanges; and C3604 brass serves instrumentation and pneumatic assemblies. Every quote includes material guidance.

Surface Treatments for
Flange Machining Components

Select finishing by corrosion, sealing-surface protection, dimensional effect on bores and faces, applicable standards, and service environment. Masking and post-finish inspection preserve critical geometry.

Passivation — ASTM A967

Removes free iron and machining contamination to support stainless corrosion resistance in process, marine, and food applications. ASTM A967 passivation can be specified for piping and pressure work, with no material effect on sealing-face finish or bore dimensions.

Electropolishing (Stainless Steel CNC Machining Flange)

Electrochemical smoothing to Ra 0.4µm for high-purity semiconductor, pharmaceutical, food, and biotechnology equipment. It improves cleanability and reduces particle or biofilm adhesion on specified stainless surfaces.

Hard Anodize — MIL-A-8625 Type III (Aluminum)

Type III hard anodise adds surface hardness above HV 400 for corrosion and wear resistance. Coating thickness is typically 25–50µm with dimensional allowance included in the machining plan for aerospace and automotive parts.

Chemical Film — MIL-DTL-5541 (Aluminum)

Conversion coating for conductive aerospace and automotive bonding, RF continuity, and ESD protection. Class 3 supports low-resistance bonding; Class 1A prioritises corrosion protection. Its minimal thickness suits tight bores and sealing faces.

Electroless Nickel (Brass Machining Precision Flange)

Uniform 25–50µm corrosion and wear protection for brass flanges in marine or aggressive chemical service. The deposit covers bores, threads, and sealing faces evenly, making it useful for saltwater cooling assemblies.

Zinc, Epoxy & Hot-Dip Galvanizing (Carbon Steel)

Hot-dip galvanising, zinc phosphate plus epoxy, and cold-zinc systems protect carbon steel outdoors, underground, and near marine service. Sealing faces and bores are masked to retain dimension and 125–250 AARH serrated-face requirements.

Passivation, electropolishing, hard anodise, conversion coating, electroless nickel, and zinc or epoxy systems are planned with masking for sealing faces and precision bores. Certificates and Ra records can be included in the shipment package.

Flange Machining FAQ

Answers to common questions about face finish, material selection, bolt-circle accuracy, quality control, quotes, and lead times.

Face flatness is central because it distributes gasket load across the full sealing area. For standard raised-face, spiral-wound gasket applications, CNCPioneer can target 0.005mm flatness. For high-pressure RTJ joints, groove width and depth can be held to ±0.025mm to support reliable ring-gasket interference.

316L stainless is preferred for direct chemical media, chlorides, sour gas, seawater, and higher-temperature service where corrosion margin is decisive. 6061-T6 or 7075-T6 aluminium is much lighter for aerospace, automotive, and automation, with anodise or chemical film for typical outdoor exposure. DFM confirms the trade-off for the actual medium and load.

BCD and individual-hole position control the uniformity of gasket loading. Standard programmes can target ±0.05mm BCD, while high-precision work can target ±0.02mm BCD and position. The required value depends on gasket type, pressure class, and bolt count.

ASME B16.5 uses a 125–250 AARH concentric serrated finish for spiral-wound gaskets, approximately Ra 3.2–6.3µm. Profilometry verifies the required texture on the first article and at planned intervals so the gasket can seat without damaging sealing reliability.

Quotes arrive within 24 hours. Typical prototypes take 5–7 business days for untreated carbon steel or 6061-T6, 7–10 for 316L, 8–12 with hard anodise, and 10–14 for titanium or Inconel. Established production is typically 3–6 weeks; new tooling, fixtures, or first PPAP/FAIR may require 6–8 weeks.

Quality plans combine MAZAK mill-turn and Swiss CNC machining, CMM verification, XRF PMI, profilometry, and calibrated thread gauges with IATF 16949 and AS9100D documentation. PPAP or FAIR samples, dimensional results, and audits can support supplier qualification. The cost advantage comes from production economics, not a reduced control plan.
