What Is a
Humanoid Robot Joint Housing?

A humanoid robot joint housing is the precision structural body around an actuated joint. It provides bearing seats, carries bending and torsional loads, connects limb segments, and manages sealing, cables, and heat. We machine housings from finger to hip, including shells, cross-roller seats, and lightweight bodies.

Bore diameter, roundness, and concentricity determine preload, friction, stiffness, and alignment. Single-setup mill-turn machining, air gauges, roundness testing, and CMM verification support ±0.002mm diameter, ±0.001mm roundness, and ±0.003mm concentricity.

  • Bearing-bore precision as a core competency H6/J6/K6 bores can reach ±0.002mm diameter, ±0.001mm roundness, and ±0.003mm bore-to-bore concentricity. Air gauges, roundness testing, and CMM verify critical features on every qualified lot.
  • Lightweight housing design and mass discipline Pocket-optimised 7075-T6 and AZ91D housings use ultrasonic wall mapping to ±0.050mm and mass verification to ±0.5g. AZ91D can reduce housing mass by 35% against 7075-T6, lowering distal inertia and actuator demand.
  • Single-setup bore concentricity preservation Sequential boring can introduce 0.010–0.030mm re-registration error and excess friction. Single-setup mill-turn programs machine both bearing bores in one chucking, using ±0.002mm machine positioning rather than rechucking uncertainty.

Five-axis setups hold compound axes to ±0.02°.

Lightweight Joint Housing — Materials & Topology

AZ91D is 35% lighter than 7075-T6 and is machined under NFPA 484 safety controls, then nickel plated within 72 hours. 7075-T6 supports five-axis pocketing to ±0.050mm, variable walls, fillets, and ribs; Ti-6Al-4V suits fatigue-critical, high-load housings. Thin-Wall Joint Housing Process Disciplines

Surface Treatments for
Humanoid Robot Joint Housings

Coating thickness directly affects bearing-bore fit. Type III anodise adds 0.015–0.050mm per side, so DFM builds growth allowance into the machined bore and verifies the delivered fit by air gauge.

Type III Hard Anodize — MIL-A-8625

Standard wear and corrosion protection for aluminium exteriors and bearing bores, with HV 400+ hardness. Black Type III supports visual continuity. Bore allowances cover 0.015–0.050mm per-side growth; post-anodise air gauges and eddy-current checks verify final H6/J6/K6 fits and controlled thickness.

Type II Clear Anodize & Powder Coat

Type II clear anodise is a thinner 5–25µm option for indoor cobots where Type III appearance is unsuitable. It offers consistent colour on 6061-T6 and lower bore-allowance risk. Alodine plus powder coat supports branded, colour-matched housings and 500+ hour salt-spray requirements.

Electroless Nickel — MIL-C-26074

Mandatory corrosion protection for AZ91D, uniformly covering bores, pockets, ribs, channels, and external walls. Plating allowances are machined in advance; eddy-current and post-plate air-gauge checks confirm coverage and H6/J6 fit. AZ91D moves from machining to plating within 72 hours under controlled humidity.

Passivation — ASTM A967

Required for 316L and 17-4PH H900 housings, flanges, and end caps. ASTM A967 passivation removes free iron and restores the chromium-oxide layer for sterilisation, saline, and humid environments. It adds no measurable dimension to ±0.002mm seats; certificates are supplied for corrosion-sensitive programmes.

DLC Coating — Bearing Bore Running Surfaces

DLC is a 1–3µm, low-friction, HV 2,000–5,000 coating for sliding bearing-bore interfaces and titanium seats prone to fretting. Wire-EDM Ra 0.2µm bores provide a suitable adhesion surface; thickness allowance is planned into the machined dimension.

Alodine Class 3 & Black Oxide

Alodine Class 3 gives designated aluminium bonding surfaces low-resistance EMC performance while anodise protects the remainder. Black oxide provides low-reflectance corrosion protection for 42CrMo4 inserts, with 1–3µm thickness and negligible impact on ±0.002mm bore tolerances.

DFM selects Type III/II anodise, powder coat, nickel, passivation, DLC, Alodine, and black oxide with treatment certificates, thickness checks, and post-treatment bore records. Bore-growth allowance is defined so the finished housing, not only the machined part, meets the required fit.

Bore Roundness Free-State Verification Protocol

±0.001mm bore roundness is meaningful only in free state, where the bearing outer ring operates. Cross-roller seats are measured at minimum-retention clamping and again after full unclamping; any bore that changes outside tolerance is rejected, even if it passed under standard chuck force.

  • Free-state re-check after unclamping
  • ±0.001mm free-state result documented

Humanoid Robot Joint Housings FAQ

Key answers on joint versus actuator housings, bore precision, light materials, fit classes, and prototype-to-production support.

An actuator housing integrates the motor and gearbox, so its critical feature is the coaxiality chain among stator and bearing seats, usually held within 0.005–0.008mm. A joint housing is the external structural body: it carries bending and torsional loads and relies on ±0.002mm bore diameter, ±0.001mm roundness, and ±0.003mm concentricity. Both use single-setup machining, but actuator housings prioritise motor-axis alignment while joint housings prioritise thin-wall free-state bore precision.

An out-of-round bore distorts the bearing outer race, concentrates rolling-element stress, and can shorten bearing life from a 10&sup8;-cycle design target to 10&sup6;–10&sup7; cycles. During operation it creates once-per-revolution stiffness and torque variation; over time, concentrated stress accelerates fatigue spalling. ±0.001mm free-state roundness keeps redistribution below the Hertzian fatigue threshold at rated load.

AZ91D requires NFPA 484 controls for combustible chips: dedicated wet-chip handling, Class D extinguishers, accumulation limits, and no compressed-air clearing. It must be nickel plated within 72 hours under controlled humidity. Bore allowances cover 8–12µm per-side nickel growth, then post-plate air gauges verify H6/J6 fits. Sharp positive-rake carbide tools and free-state finish checks are essential for precise, low-friction bores and channels.

A complete callout includes four elements: ISO class, bearing outer-ring tolerance at operating temperature, surface treatment, and finish. Use H6 for standard stationary outer rings, H7 for light-load joints, J6 for tight press fits, and K6 for vibration or impact. State "H6 after Type III anodise" when applicable, rather than only H6; Ra 0.4–0.8µm is standard, or Ra 0.2µm for surgical and precision cross-roller seats. DFM then confirms the allowance and finished fit.

First articles take 5–14 days: 7075-T6 single bores 5–7, compound-bore parts 10–14, AZ91D adds 2–3 for plating, and Ti-6Al-4V takes 8–12. Revisions take 5–12 days; pilot batches of 10–500 take 2–6 weeks; PPAP Level 3 takes 6–8. Volume programmes use 100% bore gauging, CCD above 5,000 units per year, and 2–3 week monthly releases. Unit economics improve with volume and dedicated fixturing.
