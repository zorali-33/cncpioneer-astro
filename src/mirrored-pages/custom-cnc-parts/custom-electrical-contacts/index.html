What Are Custom Engineered
Electrical Contacts?

A custom precision electrical contact is a machined or assembled conductive component that makes, breaks or maintains current flow in a relay, connector, contactor, switch, circuit breaker, slip ring or test fixture. Its material, contact-face geometry, spring force, plating and dimensional control must work together for the specified electrical and mechanical duty.

CNCPioneer reviews the operating current, voltage, switching duty, cycle life, environment, contact force and mating geometry before selecting an electrical contact manufacturing route. Swiss CNC, mill-turn, EDM, grinding, plating and assembly processes support machined contacts, contact pins, spring arms, sliding rings and contact tips with dimensional, material and electrical-performance records for OEM production.

  • Contact face geometry as the primary electrical performance discipline Contact resistance R_contact = ρ_material / (2 × a_contact) where a_contact is the Hertz contact radius. Contact face flatness (0.003mm) and surface finish (Ra 0.1μm) — which together govern the true contact area fraction at the nominal contact pressure — are the machining parameters most directly governing assembled device contact resistance. CNCPioneer's contact face machining programs achieve 0.003mm flatness and Ra 0.1μm as production standard.
  • Swiss CNC as the platform for miniature precision contact manufacturing The majority of signal-level custom electrical contacts in relays, connectors, and test probes range from Ø0.8mm to Ø8mm diameter — the size regime where guide bushing support on Swiss CNC is the only process achieving ±0.002mm diameter, ±0.001mm roundness, and Ra 0.1μm contact surface from one guide bushing position without rechucking. CNCPioneer's 78+ Swiss CNC lathes machine miniature electrical contact bodies from precious metal bar stock.
  • Precious and refractory metal machining as a material-specific competency Silver, gold, platinum, palladium, tungsten, and molybdenum are the primary electrical contact materials — all of which machine fundamentally differently from common engineering alloys. Pure silver work-hardens rapidly; tungsten requires EDM or diamond grinding; gold is extremely ductile; beryllium copper requires machining before precipitation hardening. CNCPioneer's custom electrical contacts manufacturing programs apply material-specific cutting parameters, tooling, and processing sequences for each precious and refractory contact material.
  • Material, plating and electrical test control Ag, Cu, W, Au, Pt, Pd and BeCu programs can include XRF composition checks, plating-thickness verification, CMM reports, profilometry, 4-wire contact-resistance measurements and lot traceability matched to the electrical contact specification.

01

Contact Face Geometry as Primary Electrical Performance Discipline

Contact resistance R_contact = ρ_material / (2 × a_contact) where a_contact is the Hertz contact radius. Contact face flatness (0.003mm) and surface finish (Ra 0.1μm) — which together govern the true contact area fraction at the nominal contact pressure — are the machining parameters most directly governing assembled device contact resistance. CNCPioneer's contact face machining programs achieve 0.003mm flatness and Ra 0.1μm as production standard using precision facing operations with CBN or diamond inserts, verified by CMM flatness measurement and profilometry Ra per lot.

02

Swiss CNC for Miniature Precision Contact Manufacturing

The majority of signal-level custom electrical contacts in relays, connectors, and test probes range from Ø0.8mm to Ø8mm diameter — the size regime where guide bushing support on Swiss CNC is the only process achieving ±0.002mm diameter, ±0.001mm roundness, and Ra 0.1μm contact surface from one guide bushing position without rechucking. CNCPioneer's 78+ Swiss CNC lathes machine miniature electrical contact bodies from precious metal bar stock (Au, Ag, Pd, Pt) and engineering alloy bar stock (BeCu, phosphor bronze, brass, tungsten).

03

Precious and Refractory Metal Machining Competency

Silver, gold, platinum, palladium, tungsten, and molybdenum are the primary electrical contact materials — all of which machine fundamentally differently from common engineering alloys. Pure silver work-hardens rapidly; tungsten requires EDM or diamond grinding; gold is extremely ductile; beryllium copper requires machining before precipitation hardening. CNCPioneer's custom electrical contacts manufacturing programs apply material-specific cutting parameters, tooling, and processing sequences for each precious and refractory contact material.

04

Custom Engineered Contact Assembly Programs — Complete Assembly DFM

Many custom electrical contacts programs require not just the contact body but the complete contact assembly: contact face brazed or welded to spring arm; contact tip press-fitted into carrier body; contact assembly with precious metal plating applied after machining. CNCPioneer's custom engineered contact programs include assembly-level DFM — specifying the correct brazing alloy for contact face-to-arm bonding, the correct press fit interference for contact tip retention, and the correct plating sequence for optimal electrical and mechanical performance.

05

Material Composition Verification as Quality Foundation

Electrical contact material composition directly governs contact resistance, arc erosion resistance, welding resistance, and service life. Silver-tungsten (AgW) composition variations of ±2% W content change arc erosion rate by ±15%; beryllium copper (CDA 172) Be content of 1.80–2.00% is required for correct age-hardened yield strength. CNCPioneer's SII XRF composition verification on every precious metal and refractory alloy contact material lot confirms alloy composition before any machining investment.

06

Electrical Performance and Lot Verification

Contact programs can include four-wire resistance testing, spring-force verification, XRF material composition checks, plating-thickness records, CMM reports, profilometry and Certificate of Conformance documentation. The inspection plan is matched to the contact material, mating interface, current level and required production standard.

CNC Machining Processes for
Custom Electrical Contacts

CNCPioneer's custom precision electrical contacts manufacturing process runs on 78+ Swiss CNC lathes, 66+ MAZAK mill-turn centers, MAZAK VARIAXIS 5-axis platforms, and dedicated wire EDM machining centers — with material-specific cutting parameters and processing sequences for each precious and refractory contact material.

01 · SWISS CNC

Swiss CNC — Miniature Contact Manufacturing (Ø0.8–30mm)

Swiss CNC is the primary platform for miniature custom electrical contacts — guide bushing support enables ±0.002mm dimensional accuracy at the contact face while maintaining contact body roundness ±0.001mm. Sequence for pogo pin plunger (Ø1.5mm × 8mm, brass C36000): bar stock feeding through guide bushing → rough turn to +0.050mm stock → PCD turning of contact tip profile at v_c = 250 m/min, f = 0.02mm/rev producing Ra 0.1μm → finish OD to h6 ±0.003mm → retaining groove ±0.020mm → drill internal spring cavity → part-off. Contact face surface finish by material: pure silver Ra 0.1μm with PCD; gold alloy Ra 0.1μm PCD mandatory; phosphor bronze Ra 0.1μm with PCD; tungsten EDM + diamond grind Ra 0.2μm.

02 · MAZAK

MAZAK Mill-Turn — Medium to Large Contact Bodies (Ø20–150mm)

For medium and large stationary contact bodies in contactors, circuit breakers, and industrial switch assemblies: contact body OD and bore from single chuck with concentricity ±0.003mm; contact face flatness 0.003mm from MAZAK precision facing with thermal stabilization; C-axis mounting hole pattern bolt holes ±0.010mm from contact face datum; all contact body features in one setup — no rechucking error. Standard for AgSnO₂ contactor contact bodies Ø20–80mm, relay bridge contact bars, and circuit breaker arcing contact assemblies.

03 · WIRE EDM

Wire EDM — Tungsten and Refractory Contact Machining

Tungsten (HV 3,000) and molybdenum (HV 1,500) contacts cannot be machined by conventional cutting — wire EDM is the primary shaping process. Sequence: rough profile cut oversize 0.1mm → skim pass 1 ±0.010mm profile, Ra 0.8μm → skim pass 2 ±0.005mm, Ra 0.4μm → diamond grinding for flattest faces Ra 0.1μm and 0.003mm flatness. Recast layer management: two skim EDM passes reducing recast to ≤3μm; final diamond lapping removes residual recast at contact face; metallographic section verification per lot. Critical for circuit breaker arcing contacts, spark plug contacts, and vacuum interrupter contacts.

04 · GRINDING

Precision Grinding — Contact Face Flatness

For contact face flatness specifications below 0.005mm, precision surface grinding or cylindrical grinding is the enabling process. Surface grinding for contact face flatness 0.003mm: CBN or aluminum oxide grinding wheel for silver and copper alloys; diamond for tungsten and ceramic-metal composites; water-soluble coolant for non-precious metals; DI water for precious metal contacts (preventing contamination). Stock removal 0.020–0.050mm per side final grind. Flatness verification: CMM scan 25-point grid; or optical flat interferometry for 0.001mm flatness programs.

05 · IN-PROCESS

In-Process Control & Final Inspection

Swiss CNC: first-off laser micrometer contact body OD before batch release; in-process OD monitoring every 50 pieces. MAZAK: first-off CMM contact face flatness before batch release. Wire EDM tungsten: recast layer metallographic section per EDM lot; contact face Ra profilometry after diamond lapping. BeCu programs: pre-age and post-age dimensional measurement confirming spring geometry within ±0.010mm after age hardening distortion. Plating verification: XRF plating thickness 100% measurement on precious metal plated contact programs; tape adhesion test per MIL-STD-202; Ferroxyl test for nickel undercoat porosity.

06 · DOCUMENTATION

IATF 16949 / AS9100D Documentation

Certificate of Conformance · CMM dimensional report: all contact body dimensions, contact face flatness, tip geometry · Profilometry: contact face Ra; sliding surface Ra · Material certificate: precious metal or alloy composition from SII XRF · Plating thickness certificate: XRF plating thickness measurement per plating lot · Contact resistance measurement: 4-wire milliohmmeter per contact at specified contact force · Spring force measurement at rated deflection per spring contact program · PPAP Level 3 for OEM programs · FAIR per AS9102 for aerospace and defense programs · All records retained 20 years.

Materials for Custom Precision
Electrical Contacts

Custom precision electrical contacts material selection is governed by electrical resistivity, arc erosion resistance, welding resistance, hardness, spring force requirements, and corrosion stability. Pure silver (Ag 99.9%) is the default low-current signal contact material — lowest electrical resistivity of all metals at 1.59×10⁻⁸ Ω·m.

Default Signal Contact

Pure Silver (Ag 99.9%)

Electrical resistivity: 1.59×10⁻⁸ Ω·m — the lowest of all metals (minimum contact resistance). Hardness: HV 25–30 (annealed) — soft, prone to welding under high current. Applications: signal-level contacts below 5A; low-force spring contacts; communication relay contacts. Machining: PCD tooling for Ra 0.1μm; work-hardening management with sharp tools and flood coolant. Standard for signal relay contacts, connector pins, and low-current switch contacts where minimum contact resistance is the primary design requirement.

Dry-Circuit Signal

Gold (Au 99.99%)

Electrical resistivity: 2.44×10⁻⁸ Ω·m; completely stable contact resistance (no surface oxide). Applications: dry-circuit signal contacts below 100mA; instrumentation relay contacts; connectors. Contact resistance: <10 mΩ consistently maintained over >10⁷ contact cycles. Machining: PCD tooling mandatory; chip evacuation critical for ductile gold. The standard contact material for precision signal relays, telecommunications connectors, and medical device contacts where atmospheric corrosion would degrade silver or copper contacts.

Precision Relay / Connector

Gold Alloys (AuAg, AuPd, AuPt)

Au-Ag alloys: reduced cost; similar corrosion stability to pure Au; harder than pure Au. Au-Pd alloys: best combination of corrosion stability, hardness, and contact resistance for precision relay contacts; HV 150–200. Applications: precision relay contacts; medical device signal contacts; telecommunications switch contacts. Machined in annealed condition before age hardening; PCD tooling for Ra 0.1μm contact face finish. The preferred material for high-cycle precision relays where pure gold's softness would produce excessive wear.

Sliding / Connector

Palladium (Pd) and Palladium Alloys

Pd-Ag (60/40): standard connector contact material for crimped and stamped contacts; excellent corrosion resistance at lower cost than pure Pd. Pd-Ni: superior hardness for sliding contact applications; HV 200–250. Applications: precision connector contacts; sliding contacts; encoder contacts. Machinable with carbide tooling at moderate cutting speeds; PCD for Ra 0.1μm contact face programs. The cost-effective alternative to gold for connector contacts in moderate-duty applications.

Highest Corrosion Stability

Platinum (Pt) and Platinum Alloys

Highest corrosion stability of all contact metals. Applications: precision measurement contacts; medical implant contacts; high-temperature contacts. Machining: PCD tooling mandatory due to platinum's work-hardening tendency; reduced cutting speeds versus gold. The material of choice for medical implant electrodes and high-temperature instrument contacts where no other metal provides adequate corrosion resistance and biocompatibility.

RoHS Arcing Standard

Silver-Tin Oxide (AgSnO₂ 88/12)

Composite powder metallurgy material: 88% Ag matrix with 12% SnO₂ particles. Arc erosion resistance: 3–5× better than pure Ag. RoHS compliant — the replacement for AgCdO in motor contactors. Applications: AC motor contactors 20–100A; lighting contactors; relay contacts in arcing duty. Machining: sintered billet; carbide tooling at reduced speed; Ra 0.4μm achievable; composition verified by SII XRF: Ag content ±1%; Sn content ±0.5%. The dominant arcing contact material for new RoHS-compliant AC contactor designs.

Maximum Arc Resistance

Silver-Tungsten (AgW 50/50, 60/40, 72/28)

Tungsten particle reinforcement: maximum arc erosion resistance in silver matrix. Contact resistance: higher than pure Ag but acceptable for power applications. Applications: circuit breaker contacts; high-voltage AC contactors; severe arcing duty. Machining: sintered billet; wire EDM for complex geometries; diamond grinding for contact face flatness 0.003mm. The standard material for molded case circuit breaker (MCCB) and air circuit breaker (ACB) arcing contacts.

General Purpose Switching

Silver-Nickel (AgNi 90/10)

Improved hardness and arc erosion versus pure Ag; lower contact resistance than AgW. Applications: 10–50A general switching; automotive switch contacts; moderate arc duty. Machining: sintered billet; carbide tooling; Ra 0.4μm standard; PCD for Ra 0.1μm precision programs. The practical compromise between pure silver's low contact resistance and AgW's maximum arc resistance for moderate-duty switching applications.

Medium-Duty / Brazing

Silver-Copper Alloy (AgCu 72-28, eutectic)

Improved hardness HV 60–80 versus pure Ag. Good brazing alloy — commonly used as contact face bonding material as well as contact material. Applications: medium-duty contacts 5–20A; contacts requiring better mechanical strength than pure Ag. Machinable with carbide tooling; PCD for Ra 0.1μm contact face programs. The standard brazing filler material for attaching precious metal contact tips to copper or brass carrier arms.

High-Force Spring Contacts

Beryllium Copper CDA 172 (BeCu)

Composition: Cu 97.9%; Be 1.80–2.00%; Co 0.20–0.35%. Properties after precipitation hardening (H900): yield strength 1,100–1,380 MPa; HV 350–400. Spring contact force per unit deflection: 5–10× higher than phosphor bronze. Key machining discipline: machine in annealed condition (A condition) to ±0.010mm spring geometry, THEN age harden (315°C × 3 hours) — aging produces ±0.003–0.008mm dimensional change. CNCPioneer BeCu program: machine in A condition ±0.020mm; verify post-age ±0.010mm. Applications: spring contact pins, test probe contacts, connector spring contacts, high-reliability relay spring contacts.

Cost-Effective Spring

Phosphor Bronze CDA 510

Composition: Cu 94.8%; Sn 5.0%; P 0.2%. Properties: yield strength 510 MPa (H08 condition); HV 150. Cost-effective spring contact material for moderate force applications. Applications: standard connector spring contacts; switch spring contacts where BeCu spring force not required. Machinable with carbide tooling; PCD for Ra 0.1μm contact face programs. The economical alternative to BeCu for connector and switch spring contacts in moderate-duty applications.

Most Machinable Copper

Brass C36000 (Free-Machining Brass)

The most machinable copper alloy — 100% machinability rating. Applications: contact body structural elements; pogo pin barrels; contact carrier bodies; connector pin structural elements (with precious metal plating applied). Plating: nickel undercoat 1.5–3.0μm + gold topcoat 0.3–3.0μm for connector contact programs. Machinable with standard carbide tooling at high cutting speeds; PCD for Ra 0.1μm contact face programs. The standard structural material for plated connector contacts and pogo pin housings.

Maximum Arc Resistance

Tungsten (W, pure)

HV 3,000; melting point 3,422°C; lowest vapor pressure of all metals. Maximum arc erosion resistance. Wire EDM primary machining process; diamond finish grinding for contact face. Applications: circuit breaker arcing contacts; spark plug contacts; vacuum interrupter contacts. Recast layer management: two skim EDM passes reducing recast to ≤3μm; final diamond lapping removes residual recast at contact face; metallographic section verification per lot. The ultimate arcing contact material for extreme current interruption duty.

Vacuum Interrupter

Molybdenum (Mo, pure)

HV 1,500; excellent arc resistance; good thermal conductivity. Applications: vacuum interrupter moving contacts; high-energy arcing contacts. Surface finish Ra 0.2μm from diamond grinding; flatness 0.003mm from precision lapping. The preferred material for vacuum interrupter contacts where tungsten's extreme hardness would produce excessive mating contact wear.

Wafer Probe Tip

Tungsten-Rhenium Alloy (W-Re 3%)

Hardness HV 500+ and minimum tip wear during silicon scrub. Applications: semiconductor wafer probing cantilever probe tips; fine-pitch test probe contacts. Machining: precision wire EDM + centerless grinding to ±0.002mm diameter; tip radius grinding to ±5μm. The standard tip material for semiconductor parametric test probes where tip wear resistance and scrub consistency determine test accuracy.

Surface Treatments for
Custom Electrical Contacts

Custom precision electrical contacts surface treatment selection addresses contact resistance stability, arc erosion resistance, corrosion protection, wear resistance, and solderability — with plating thickness controlled to ±0.1μm and masking of non-contact zones to prevent unintended plating deposition.

Gold (Au) Plating — Electrodeposited

The standard contact surface finish for low-level signal contacts in relays, connectors, and switches. Hard gold: Au-Co alloy (0.1–0.3% Co); HV 130–200; most common for connector contacts and relay contacts; ASTM B488 Type II, Grade C (0.75μm minimum for class 1 connectors; 1.27μm for class 2). Soft gold: pure Au (99.9%); HV 60–80; for wire bonding pads and connector contacts requiring maximum conductivity. Flash gold: 0.05–0.25μm thin gold on nickel undercoat — for press-fit contacts and tarnish protection. Nickel undercoat: 1.5–5.0μm electroless or electrolytic nickel under all gold plating — provides diffusion barrier preventing copper substrate migration to gold surface over time.

Palladium-Nickel (PdNi) Plating

The best balance of cost, hardness, and corrosion stability for connector contacts that cannot use gold due to cost. PdNi 80/20 composition; HV 450–550 — harder than gold, providing superior wear resistance for sliding and wiping connector contacts. Tarnish resistance: excellent — Pd does not form stable tarnish films like Ag. Applications: automotive connector contacts, telecommunications connector contacts, consumer electronics connector contacts. Custom Electrical Contact Manufacturing FAQ

Common questions from relay and contactor manufacturers, circuit breaker OEMs, connector system producers, automotive switch manufacturers, and medical device contact suppliers about CNCPioneer's custom precision electrical contacts manufacturing capability, material selection, surface finish, and beryllium copper processing.

Low-current dry-circuit signal relay contacts — operating below 100mA at voltages below 28V — present the most demanding material selection challenge in custom electrical contacts manufacturing because at these current levels, the electrical arc that self-cleans contact surfaces during switching is absent. High-current contacts (above 5A) benefit from arc energy that periodically evaporates surface oxide and organic films from the contact face, maintaining a metallic contact surface regardless of the contact material's tendency to form surface films. Below 100mA and 28V, no arc occurs on contact opening — every contact film that forms on the contact face (silver oxide Ag₂O; silver sulfide Ag₂S; organic condensates from atmosphere) remains on the surface permanently and adds series resistance to the contact's baseline metallic resistance. After 10,000 contact cycles without arc cleaning, a silver contact in a standard industrial atmosphere accumulates 0.5–2.0 nm of Ag₂S tarnish that raises contact resistance from an initial 20 mΩ to 500 mΩ–2,000 mΩ — sufficient to cause logic circuit false-output from the contact's excessive voltage drop. The correct material for dry-circuit signal relay contacts: gold (pure or alloy) or palladium alloy. Pure gold (Au 99.99%) forms no stable oxide under any atmospheric condition — contact resistance remains within 10 mΩ of initial value through 10⁷+ contact cycles in normal industrial atmosphere. Gold-palladium alloys (Au-Pd 60/40 or 70/30) add palladium hardness (HV 150–200 versus HV 60–80 for pure gold) for improved wear resistance in high-cycle contacts while maintaining gold's corrosion stability. Ruthenium (Ru) plating on silver or gold is used for extremely high-cycle applications (>10⁷ cycles) — Ru's HV 700–900 hardness provides maximum wear resistance with adequate corrosion stability for dry-circuit use. CNCPioneer's DFM review for every custom electrical contacts program includes this current-level-based material selection analysis — recommending the correct precious metal alloy for the customer's operating current, voltage, cycle life, and atmospheric exposure conditions.

Ra 0.1μm on silver and gold contact faces is achieved by single-pass PCD (polycrystalline diamond) turning at optimized cutting parameters: v_c = 300–500 m/min for silver (10–20× higher than standard carbide cutting speed); feed rate f = 0.02–0.03mm/rev (below the nose radius-governed Ra threshold for the 0.2mm nose radius PCD insert used); depth of cut 0.020–0.050mm (single finish pass, no re-entry roughness artifact). At these parameters, the tool-workpiece contact is in the hydrodynamic cutting regime where the chip separates cleanly from the substrate without the built-up edge and stick-slip mechanisms that produce Ra 0.4–0.8μm from standard carbide tooling. The critical importance of Ra 0.1μm for contact resistance: contact resistance R = ρ/(2a) depends on the true contact area through the effective contact radius a. Two contacting surfaces with Ra 0.4μm asperities develop approximately 200 asperity contact spots per mm² at 1N contact force, with effective contact radius a_total ≈ 15μm. At Ra 0.1μm, 800 asperity contacts per mm² develop at the same 1N force, with a_total ≈ 30μm — the smoother surface produces 2× larger effective contact radius and 2× lower constriction resistance (R = ρ/(2a) halves when a doubles). For a silver contact: R at Ra 0.4μm ≈ 530 mΩ; R at Ra 0.1μm ≈ 265 mΩ — a 2× contact resistance reduction purely from surface finish improvement at constant contact force. For precision signal relay contacts where target contact resistance is ≤25 mΩ (achieved by gold plating providing oxide-free contact), the substrate surface finish under the gold plating must be Ra ≤0.2μm to ensure the gold plating deposits conformally rather than following asperity peaks — non-conformal gold plating on rough substrates has gold thickness variation that exposes substrate copper or nickel at asperity peaks faster than on a smooth substrate, reducing service life.

Beryllium copper (CDA 172, 1.9% Be) spring contacts achieve their high spring force through precipitation hardening — the metallurgical transformation from annealed (solution treated, A condition: yield strength ≈ 200 MPa) to aged (H900 condition: yield strength ≈ 1,100–1,380 MPa) that converts the initially soft, ductile alloy into a high-strength spring material. The processing sequence that CNCPioneer's BeCu spring contact programs follow is: machine in A (annealed) condition → age harden → verify post-age dimensions and spring force → plate. This sequence is mandatory because age hardening (3 hours at 315°C / 600°F) produces predictable dimensional changes — typically 0.003–0.008mm shrinkage on machined OD features from the precipitation-induced lattice contraction — that can be pre-compensated in the machining program. If machining were attempted after age hardening, the material's HV 350–400 hardness would require carbide at HSM parameters, and the achievable surface finish would degrade to Ra 0.8–1.6μm versus Ra 0.4μm from annealed BeCu — unacceptable for spring contact surface programs. The pre-age machining dimensions are adjusted for predicted age hardening distortion: for a spring arm 0.500mm thick with expected 0.005mm shrinkage in the thickness direction, CNCPioneer machines to 0.505mm in A condition — after aging, the arm thickness shrinks to 0.500mm ±0.003mm. Post-age dimensional verification confirms that every BeCu spring contact is within ±0.010mm of final drawing specification before plating investment. The age hardening is performed at qualified heat treatment facilities with furnace temperature uniformity ±5°C, documented furnace charts, and hardness verification (Rockwell C 38–44 for H900 CDA 172) on every BeCu contact lot — the metallurgical quality documentation that ensures the designed spring force is achieved in every assembled contact.
