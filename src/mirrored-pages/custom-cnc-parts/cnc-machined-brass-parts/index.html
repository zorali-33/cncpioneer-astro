What Is CNC Machined
Brass Parts Manufacturing?

CNC machined brass parts manufacturing is the precision computer-controlled turning, milling, Swiss-type turning, thread cutting, knurling, and drilling process — executed on 78+ Swiss CNC lathes with guide bushing support, 66+ MAZAK mill-turn centers with live tooling and C-axis, and MAZAK VARIAXIS 5-axis platforms — producing the wide diversity of functional metallic components from copper-zinc alloy whose machinability, conductivity, corrosion resistance, aesthetics, and cost represent the optimal material choice across plumbing, electrical, automotive, lock hardware, instrumentation, hydraulic, and consumer industries.

Brass is the most widely used material for CNC precision turning parts after aluminum. C36000 free-machining brass at machinability index 100% is the universal reference standard — 3× that of steel — enabling cutting speeds of 300–600 m/min from PCD tooling, production rates of 2,000–5,000 parts per hour on standard Ø8–20mm programs, and the wholesale pricing that OEM supply chains require. The combination of machinability, 26% IACS conductivity, corrosion resistance, and gold-yellow aesthetics makes brass the dominant material for pipe fittings, lock hardware, electrical terminals, and decorative components globally.

Brass Alloys for
CNC Machined Brass Parts

Brass alloy selection is governed by three criteria in priority order: regulatory compliance for the specific end-use market (potable water, RoHS, marine), dezincification resistance from service environment, and machinability economics. C36000 covers the majority of non-regulated applications at best machining cost; DZR/CW602N is mandatory for UK/EU/AU potable water; C26000 for lead-free electrical and medical.

C36000 · Most Common · 100% Machinability

C36000 Free-Machining Brass

Cu 60–63% · Pb 2.5–3.7% · Zn balance · UTS 340–469 MPa · HRB 55–80 · 26% IACS. The machinability reference standard. Best cost for non-regulated industrial, commercial, and general consumer applications. v_c = 400–600 m/min PCD.

DZR CW602N · Potable Water · WRAS

DZR Brass (CW602N)

Cu 62–65% · Pb 1.6–2.5% · As 0.02–0.06% (XRF-confirmed) · Zn balance. Mandatory for UK/EU/AU potable water fittings per BS EN 12164. WRAS-compliant. Identical machinability to C36000 (100%). +8–12% material premium.

C26000 · Lead-Free · RoHS

C26000 Cartridge Brass (70/30)

Cu 68.5–71.5% · Pb ≤0.07% · Zn balance · UTS 330–620 MPa · Machinability 30%. Lead-free for RoHS-regulated electrical and medical applications. Excellent cold-forming and corrosion resistance. PCD tooling required.

C46400 · Naval Brass · Marine

C46400 Naval Brass

Cu 59–62% · Sn 0.5–1.0% · Zn balance · UTS 379–586 MPa · Machinability 40%. Tin provides dezincification resistance without arsenic addition. Specify for marine hardware, sea water valve bodies, and boat fittings.

BeCu C17200 · Spring Contact

Beryllium Copper C17200 AT

Cu 97.3% · Be 1.8% · Co 0.5% · UTS 1,380 MPa · Yield 1,200 MPa · HRC 40. Highest-strength copper alloy. Precision spring contacts, high-stress mechanism parts. Wet machining mandatory (BeCu dust carcinogen).

C51000 · Phosphor Bronze · Spring

Phosphor Bronze C51000

Cu 94.8% · Sn 5.0% · P 0.2% · UTS 310–790 MPa · Machinability 20%. Excellent spring properties, fatigue resistance, and corrosion resistance. Precision spring turned bodies, bearing sleeves, electrical spring contact bodies.

48-Hour DFM Alloy Selection: CNCPioneer's DFM identifies the market (UK/EU/AU = DZR mandatory for water contact; US with NSF/ANSI 61 = lead-free verification; industrial = C36000 standard), service environment (potable water, aggressive chemistry, sea water, general industrial), and regulatory documentation required (WRAS approval, NSF/ANSI 61, CE marking). In practice: C36000's 100% machinability versus C26000's 30% produces 2–3× lower machining cost — specifying the correct alloy is both a compliance and an economics decision.

CNC Machined Brass Parts FAQ

Common questions from plumbing and HVAC fitting OEMs, automotive Tier 1 brass parts suppliers, electrical and electronics connector manufacturers, lock and security hardware OEMs, and precision engineering distributors about CNCPioneer's CNC machined brass parts capability, alloy selection, thread standards, knurl quality, surface treatment, and wholesale program economics.

Brass alloy selection is governed by three criteria in priority order. First, regulatory compliance — non-negotiable: in the UK, EU (Drinking Water Directive 2020/2184), Australia (AS/NZS 4020), and US states adopting NSF/ANSI 61 lead-content requirements, brass fittings in contact with potable drinking water must use DZR brass (CW602N) with arsenic content 0.02–0.06% confirmed by chemical analysis. For these markets, C36000 (3% lead, no dezincification inhibitor) cannot be used for water-contact fittings regardless of superior machinability. For RoHS-regulated EU electrical/electronic applications, C36000's 3% lead technically requires an RoHS exemption (currently exempted under Annex III below 4% by weight — C36000 at 3% is within this exemption); however, OEMs with corporate lead-reduction commitments increasingly specify lead-free C26000 even when the RoHS exemption applies. Second, dezincification resistance: for applications in softened water, chlorinated water above 300 ppm, heated water above 60°C, or acidic water pH <7 — even where regulation does not mandate DZR — C36000's zinc can leach from the brass matrix producing a porous copper sponge that weakens the fitting. DZR brass (As inhibitor) suppresses this mechanism and should be specified for any brass fitting in aggressive water chemistry service. Third, machinability economics: for non-regulated industrial and commercial applications, C36000's 100% machinability versus C26000's 30% produces 2–3× lower machining cost — the wholesale pricing that high-volume programs require. CNCPioneer's 48-hour DFM identifies the market (UK/EU/AU = DZR mandatory; US with NSF/ANSI 61 = lead-free verification; industrial = C36000), service environment, and regulatory documentation required before machining commitment.

Consistent knurl quality at high production volume requires controlling three distinct mechanisms. First, knurl pattern tracking: for a diamond knurl pattern to track perfectly, the workpiece circumference must be an exact multiple of the knurl wheel tooth pitch. For a 0.8mm pitch diamond knurl, Ø7.64mm (circumference = π × 7.64 = 24.00mm = 30 × 0.8mm) tracks correctly; Ø7.70mm (circumference = 24.19mm = 30.24 × 0.8mm) produces a wandering "drunken knurl" pattern from 0.24 tooth-pitch non-closure after 30 teeth. CNCPioneer calculates the workpiece OD target from the knurl pitch for each new program — when the customer's designed OD is not a tracking circumference, CNCPioneer calculates the nearest tracking OD (typically within ±0.15mm of nominal) and confirms it falls within dimensional tolerance before proceeding. Second, knurl depth consistency: servo-controlled radial force on Swiss CNC and MAZAK mill-turn live tooling knurl programs ensures repeatable depth versus spring-loaded approaches that vary from setup to setup; material hardness verification per lot allows knurl force compensation for C36000 HRB 55–80 hardness variation that would otherwise produce 20–30% depth variation. Third, knurl wheel wear management: wheels replaced at defined wear intervals (sharp teeth: flat-to-peak profile with sharp peak; worn: rounded peak and reduced effective depth); in-process visual inspection every 50 pieces; depth spot measurement every 100 pieces. Pre-knurl OD is calculated from post-knurl diameter target incorporating C36000 brass diameter growth of approximately 0.05–0.15mm per 0.1mm knurl depth from material displaced outward — preventing the common error of targeting post-knurl OD at the pre-knurl turning OD and delivering parts 0.05–0.15mm oversized after knurling. Knurl acceptance: depth ±0.050mm precision; 100% visual per lot for pattern regularity, clean start/stop zones, and absence of micro-cracking at knurl peaks.

CNCPioneer's brass pipe fitting thread programs cover the complete international thread standard portfolio. NPT (National Pipe Taper, ASME B1.20.1): 1/8"–4" taper at 1:16 on diameter, 60° thread angle; L1 hand-tight engagement length per ASME B1.20.1 Table 2; NIST-traceable NPT thread gauge verification. BSP parallel (G thread, ISO 228/EN 10226-2): G1/8–G4 parallel; O-ring or washer face seal — standard for European, UK, and most Asian non-North American markets. BSP taper (R thread, ISO 7-1/EN 10226-1): R1/8–R4 tapered at 1:16 with Whitworth 55° thread angle; self-sealing like NPT but with Whitworth form — UK, Australia, many Asian and Middle East markets. DIN 2999: German/ISO tapered pipe thread for German machinery and European industrial plumbing. ISO metric M3–M100 external and internal; JIC 37° and SAE 45° flare; UNF/UNC. Thread quality verification: 100% calibrated GO/NO-GO gauge per production lot on every threaded brass part — no sampling exceptions. This is non-negotiable for pipe fitting programs where thread quality determines hydraulic sealing: a GO gauge confirms the thread is not undersized (thread will engage properly); a NO-GO gauge confirms the thread is not oversized (thread will not engage past the designed stop point). Gauge calibration per ISO 10012:2003 with NIST-traceable calibration records; gauge wear monitoring with replacement at defined intervals. Post-plating 100% thread re-gauging confirms plating thickness did not close threaded bores below minimum engagement.
