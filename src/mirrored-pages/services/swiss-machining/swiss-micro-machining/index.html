Micro Swiss Machining Services

Micro swiss machining is a high-precision process that removes small amounts of material using cutting tools. Unlike conventional machining, it uses a guide bushing that supports the workpiece very close to the tool. This short distance reduces deflection and vibration, enabling higher accuracy and finer surface finishes.

FAQs

What is the main difference between Micro Swiss Machining and standard Swiss screw machining?

Micro Swiss Machining is an ultra-specialized version of Swiss machining that focuses on even smaller diameters (often below Ø1–3 mm), tighter tolerances (down to ±0.0001″ / ±2–3 μm), and more intricate micro-features that push beyond what conventional Swiss machines can reliably achieve.

What kinds of parts and industries benefit most from Micro Swiss Machining?

It excels at producing tiny, high-precision components such as micro bone screws, surgical needles, dental implants, catheter components, micro connectors, sensor housings, watch jewels, micro shafts/pins, and miniature robotics parts — primarily serving medical, dental, aerospace, defense, electronics, and high-end watchmaking industries.

What tolerances and minimum feature sizes can Micro Swiss Machining realistically achieve?

Standard capability is ±0.0002″ (±5 μm), with many shops routinely hitting ±0.0001″ (±2–3 μm) or better on critical dimensions. Minimum turned diameters often go down to 0.1–0.3 mm, and micro-features (slots, grooves, cross-holes) can be as small as 0.05–0.1 mm depending on material and geometry.
